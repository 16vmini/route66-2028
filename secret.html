The Idea

Drive Route 66 in a rental. Somewhere along the way, fall in love with a classic American car. Buy it (or arrange to buy it). Ship it home. Park it in the drive in Britain.

Sounds like a fantasy. It's not — people do this every year, and the maths works out cheaper than buying the same car already-imported in the UK.

Why It's Actually Possible

  • classic relief Cars 30+ years old at time of UK import qualify as "of historical interest" — 5% VAT only, no import duty (vs. 10% duty + 20% VAT on a modern car).
  • no IVA test Vehicles 30+ years old are exempt from the Individual Vehicle Approval test.
  • no MOT Vehicles 40+ years old are MOT-exempt as Vehicles of Historic Interest (still must be roadworthy).
  • dry-state advantage Cars from Texas, Arizona, New Mexico, California — exactly where Route 66 runs — are notoriously rust-free. The route itself is a giant classic-car catalogue.
  • left-hand drive is fine Permitted on UK roads. Insurance is slightly higher but available from classic specialists (Footman James, Hagerty UK, Adrian Flux).

Where to Look

Online — best for buying remotely

  • Bring a Trailer — auction format, very high quality listings, strong vetting. The default for serious classic-car buyers.
  • Hemmings — long-running classic-car marketplace, includes dealers and private listings.
  • Classic.com — price tracking across auctions; great for understanding fair market value.
  • eBay Motors — wider but more variable; buy with caution.

In person — during the trip

  • Texas Panhandle (Day 6): Amarillo and Lubbock area dealers. Lots of dry-stored classics.
  • New Mexico / Arizona (Days 7–11): Dry climate gold. Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Flagstaff have specialist dealers.
  • Southern California (Days 13–17): The biggest classic-car market in the US. Lots of dealers in LA / Orange County. Walk-in viewings possible.
  • Heads up: Mecum Glendale auction typically runs in late March in Arizona — could be a perfectly-timed Day 11 detour. Check their 2028 calendar.

Getting It Home

Shipping

  • Roll-on / Roll-off (RoRo) — drive on, drive off the ship. Cheaper (~$1,800–$3,000) but car is exposed to other vehicles. Fine for sturdy classics, less ideal for show cars.
  • Containerised (20ft / 40ft shared) — secure, weatherproof. ~$3,500–$6,500 LA → Southampton/Felixstowe. Worth it for valuable cars.
  • Typical ports: LA (Long Beach) → Southampton or Felixstowe. Transit ~3–5 weeks.
  • Recommended brokers: West Coast Shipping, K International Transport, Schumacher Cargo Logistics. Get 3 quotes.

UK customs & registration

  1. NOVA — Notification of Vehicle Arrival to HMRC within 14 days of import.
  2. Pay duty / VAT at clearance. 30+ year cars: 5% VAT only. Newer: 10% duty + 20% VAT.
  3. Dating certificate from a recognised owners' club (e.g. Mustang Owners Club GB) for older vehicles.
  4. DVLA registration — V55/5 form, vehicle inspection, age-related plate.
  5. Insurance — classic policy via Footman James / Hagerty / Adrian Flux. Often £200–£500/yr with limited mileage.

Risks & Showstoppers

  • condition A bad buy 5,000 miles away is a disaster. Always pay for a pre-purchase inspection ($200–$400) from a third-party specialist, not the seller's "guy".
  • title issues US states have different title rules; salvage / rebuilt titles can be unimportable. Verify clean title before paying.
  • currency A ~10% £/$ swing wipes out the savings. Book FX forwards or use Wise to lock rates if budget is tight.
  • UK rules change HMRC rules for classics have shifted before. Check current relief rates closer to the date.
  • she finds out Have a story ready. "It was meant to be a surprise" is your only defence.
